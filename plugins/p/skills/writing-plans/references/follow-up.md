# Does the plan help execution?

Evaluate the plan through work performed from it. A complete-looking document,
more checked boxes, fewer words, or fewer questions is not itself a useful outcome.
Keep study records and session evidence outside repositories. Use the existing
reviewing-improvement-effects skill when its provenance and comparison records
serve the study; its history capability gate applies before reading session data.
For a direct repair, a small private evidence note may suffice.

Before a comparison, preserve the planning instructions and resulting plan
versions, the capable existing approach, task eligibility and outcome checks.
Compare like tasks and executor capabilities. Identify actual instruction use;
an installed file alone does not prove exposure. Keep development cases separate
from subsequent confirmation. The executor needs the requirements as well as the
plan, so plan mistakes cannot silently redefine success.

Follow consecutive eligible requests through execution, including nonuse,
abandonment, failure and unresolved outcomes. For each, retain enough evidence to
answer:

- Was the requested outcome actually delivered and later used?
- What required rework? Was its cause an avoidable planning omission, executor
  error, an unavailable fact, or a legitimate change in requirements?
- Did a dependency or assumption check prevent a concrete wrong action? Which
  evidence supports that judgment, and what would have happened is still unknown?
- Did adaptation preserve valid work and requirements? Did a shortcut remove
  waste without dropping an obligation?
- What did planning, review, execution and repair cost separately, and what
  requester feedback establishes usability or preference?

Use verified delivery and consequential avoidable rework as candidate outcome
measures, protecting correctness and scope separately. Define the unit and
classification before grading, with unknown attribution allowed. Include all
eligible attempts in the denominator; report missing follow-up and observation
horizons. Count requirements omitted from a terse plan as well as defects in
detailed plans. If the comparison lacks credible matching or independent
outcomes, report feasibility or attributed observations, not causal improvement.

Choose a review trigger from actual work arrival: completion of a bounded set of
eligible tasks, a consequential regression, or a date to check for missing
follow-up. Record the owner, inputs and next decision. A date in a note does not
wake an agent; any actual scheduler needs its own authorized execution surface,
bounded collection and an observed run. Do not install one merely to record a
follow-up. At review, keep, revise, revert or retain insufficient evidence with a
concrete next action. No new eligible work means no new effectiveness result;
back off repeated inspection until evidence can change the decision.
