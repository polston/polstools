# General method

Start from the requested outcome and the subject's actual state. Map consequential
unknowns to plausible observations. Inspect established approaches and contrary
evidence before narrowing to a favorite. Separate exploration from confirmation:
initial error analysis may change the rubric, but confirmation binds the criteria
before results are seen.

For a proposed intervention, state its mechanism, predicted benefit, plausible
harm, comparison and what could refute it. A new domain may require researching
its measurement conventions first. Keep expertise claims limited until appropriate
methods and evidence are established. Use qualitative decision comparisons when
probabilities or utility scales lack a basis. Ask what plausible observation
would reverse the choice; choose a targeted probe when that observation matters.

Stop a branch when refuted, dominated under current requirements, infeasible or
uninformative. Preserve useful alternatives with a revisit condition. A supported
no-change outcome is valid; a convenient lack of evidence is not evidence that
nothing can improve. At the investigation bound, distinguish the strongest
supported result from unverified remaining work.
